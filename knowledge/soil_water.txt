Soil Organic Carbon, Water and Land Degradation

Agricultural soils with depleted organic matter can have reduced ability to hold water and nutrients, reduced rainfall infiltration, greater compaction and loss of soil biodiversity.

Conservation practices that maintain organic matter and soil cover can improve soil functions. FAO describes soil organic matter as important for soil health, water retention, nutrient availability and biological activity.

Source:
FAO, Soil Organic Carbon Accumulation and Greenhouse Gas Emission Reductions from Conservation Agriculture.
https://www.fao.org/4/i2672e/i2672e.pdf

Key relationships:
- Low soil organic matter can reduce water-holding capacity.
- Reduced water retention can increase vulnerability during rainfall shortages.
- Soil degradation can affect soil biodiversity.
- Maintaining soil cover and organic inputs can support soil structure and water-related functions.
