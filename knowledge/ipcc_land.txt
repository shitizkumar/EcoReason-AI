Climate Change, Land Management and Biodiversity

The IPCC identifies land ecosystems and biodiversity as vulnerable to climate change and climate extremes. Sustainable land management can help address multiple environmental stressors.

The IPCC identifies practices including cover crops, crop residue retention, reduced or zero tillage and maintenance of ground cover as options for reducing vulnerability to soil erosion and nutrient loss.

The IPCC also reports that agroforestry, perennial systems and improved land management can reduce erosion and nutrient leaching while building soil carbon.

Source:
IPCC, Special Report on Climate Change and Land, Summary for Policymakers.
https://www.ipcc.ch/srccl/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/

Additional source:
IPCC AR6 Working Group III, Chapter 7: Agriculture, Forestry and Other Land Uses.
https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-7/

Key relationships:
- Climate stress + land degradation → increased ecosystem vulnerability.
- Cover crops → reduced erosion and nutrient loss.
- Agroforestry → soil carbon + erosion reduction + water-related benefits.
- Land management can influence several environmental variables simultaneously.
