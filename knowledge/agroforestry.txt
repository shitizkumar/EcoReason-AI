Agroforestry, Soil, Water and Biodiversity

Agroforestry integrates trees and shrubs with crops and/or livestock. It can provide multiple environmental functions including improved soil cover, reduced erosion, improved soil fertility and biological activity, increased organic carbon, more effective use of available water and enhanced biodiversity.

FAO describes agroforestry systems as contributing to soil fertility, water conservation, carbon capture and biodiversity conservation.

Agroforestry can influence water cycling through interception, transpiration and soil water retention. Trees and vegetation can also reduce surface runoff and increase infiltration.

Key relationships:
- Agroforestry → soil organic matter and carbon.
- Agroforestry → soil structure and infiltration.
- Agroforestry → water retention and reduced runoff.
- Agroforestry → habitat structure and biodiversity.
